### What the startup check and setup look at

The spec's startup check refused a superuser, a role with CREATEROLE or BYPASSRLS, a member of such a role or of neon_superuser, a member of the tables' owner, UPDATE, DELETE, TRUNCATE or TRIGGER on `audit_events`, and triggers not defined exactly as setup makes them. After the reviews, the server stops at startup with `SchemaNotReadyError` (`NOT_READY`) unless:

- the schema version is right, and `audit_events` has exactly its two triggers as setup makes them (definition, enabled ALWAYS so they fire in every `session_replication_role`, calling the unchanged function: body, language, `search_path` setting, not SECURITY DEFINER), and no other trigger, rule, row security, parent or child table (the other tables aren't checked this way);
- the server's role can use schema `public` and read `schema_info`;

and with `AppRoleTooPowerfulError` if PUBLIC may create in the database or in any of its non-temporary schemas (`PUBLIC_MAY_CREATE`, which says who can take that back), or if the role the session logged in as, the role it is now, or any role either belongs to (inheriting its rights or able to SET ROLE to it) (`TOO_POWERFUL`):

- is a superuser, has CREATEROLE or BYPASSRLS, is `neon_superuser`, or is one of `pg_read_server_files`, `pg_write_server_files` and `pg_execute_server_program`;
- owns the database, any schema, or a table or function in `public`;
- may call a SECURITY DEFINER function in `public` that isn't an extension's, or create objects in the database or any non-temporary schema;
- has UPDATE on any column of `audit_events`, or DELETE, TRUNCATE or TRIGGER on it.

Setup runs with the owner's rights, so it pins its search path, calls Postgres's functions by their full names with exact argument types in its queries (its table definitions use only functions and operators Postgres has an exact match for), and stops with `SetupRefusedError`, changing nothing, if `public` holds a table or function another role owns (extension members, composite types and indexes excepted) or a trigger on a table other than `audit_events` that calls another role's function (`SETUP_FOREIGN_OBJECTS`), checking this again after creating its tables, with them locked, just before the only statement that could fire a trigger; or if PUBLIC may still create in the database afterwards (`SETUP_PUBLIC_MAY_CREATE`). It refuses a reserved app role name (`public`, `none`, `pg_...`), takes back CREATE on `public` and on the database from the app role, and leaves the caller's search path as it found it.

### Measured sizes and the final limits

Measured with two scripts kept out of the repo (`local/measure_sizes.py`, `local/measure_churn.py`), each on a throwaway Postgres 18.6 (8 kB pages, autovacuum on) set up by `schema.setup_database` as the tables' owner, as on Neon. Neon's free plan has 1 GB per project, a decimal GB, since 2026-10-02, and writes that would make storage grow fail beyond it (read on 2026-11-05: neon.com/docs/changelog/2026-10-02, neon.com/blog/neon-free-plan-1-gb-per-project, neon.com/docs/introduction/plans, neon.com/faqs/free-plan-limits-and-quotas, neon.com/docs/introduction/usage-calculations). The budget is half of it, 500,000,000 bytes.

| Part of one team, at its worst | How it was measured | Bytes |
|---|---|---|
| 102,000 audit records (100,000 writes, 2,000 reads) | 344.9 bytes a row: 102,000 of the largest rows the server writes (64-character client ID, 23-character target, every optional field filled), from 10 teams in turn | 35,176,448 |
| 1,000 invites | 456.3 bytes a used invite: 10,000 from 10 teams in turn | 456,294 |
| 1,000 cards | a full deck at the 200,000-character limit in 4-byte characters (1,277,952), plus what adding 16 cards and deleting 15, over and over with ever higher topics, leaves on a shared server: 2,785,280 a team, with 10 teams taking turns over their first 31,000 writes each (one team alone, over its whole allowance: 2,523,136, of which 163,840 came after its first 31,000 writes) | 4,063,232 |
| 25 members | the members table as the real store left it (25 × 904.4-byte rows would be less) | 131,072 |
| the team's row | the teams table as the real store left it, its one row updated about 100,000 times | 139,264 |
| **one team** | | **39,966,310** |

- **12 teams:** 500,000,000 ÷ 39,966,310 = 12.5, leaving 1.7 MB a team of room under 41,666,666. In half of 5 GB, 62 would fit; in half of 10 GB, 125. A personal team (no invites) comes to 39,510,016.
- **The real store, three ways:** one team driven through `PostgresCardStore` as the app role (the startup check on) until both allowances were used: 24 members joining; then either 1,000 cards added and graded, followed by invites up to the cap and grades (4a) or by grades only (4b), or the add-and-delete churn from the start, with no grades (4c); then the reserve used up changing a role, and 2,000 reads of the log. VACUUM ran every 3,000 calls, as autovacuum would about once a minute at the most a full team can do, and the store's clock moved a day every 400 calls, so the daily limit didn't stop the owner (25 members could make the same 100,000 changes in eight days). Each team grew the database by 29.1 to 30.8 MB, under the 39,966,310 above.
- **Why the topic index went:** with the (team_id, topic_key) index, the churn left 9.2 MB a team on a shared server, 7.3 MB of it in that index's half-empty pages, which VACUUM never merges (1.9 MB without the index). Cards are always looked up within one team, at most 1,000 of them, so the index wasn't needed. The first churn measurement used one-letter questions and answers, so it mostly measured the indexes; the numbers above use full-size cards.
- **The earlier measurement, on step 2's limits** (2026-11-05): 478 bytes an audit row with a 200-character client ID, invites without a cap, and the churn on the topic index meant 4 shared or 8 personal teams fit (5 or 9 before the churn was counted). That led to this step's limits.
- **Not counted:** Postgres's write-ahead log, and Neon's 6-hour history, which has a 1 GB limit of its own; Neon's pages on the history window, branch restore and cost optimization (read on 2026-11-05) don't say what happens past it or whether it counts toward storage (see the README's Not handled). Two things a review of the first measurement pointed out were estimated, not measured: sign-in identities (issuer and subject), whose length the server doesn't limit (about 0.2 MB more a team), and topic keys, which casefolding can make longer than their topics (about 0.15 MB). Both fit in the budget's other half.

### How the recorded run went

The run started at 20:18, once five checks a minute apart had each found no other mutation run on the machine and the CPU less than half busy, and a script noted the machine's state every minute until it ended at 04:24 the next morning.
